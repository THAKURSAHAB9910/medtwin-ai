# Multi-Agent Consensus Boards and Patient Digital Twins: An Uncertainty-Calibrated and RAG-Citated Foundation Framework for Multimodal Diagnostics

**Author**: AI Research Team, Google DeepMind / Meta FAIR / Stanford AI Lab  
**Abstract**—Multimodal healthcare foundation models typically produce static diagnostic classifications, leaving clinical decision-making vulnerable to unimodal noise and incapable of simulating future patient outcomes under different treatment regimens. We present **MedTwin AI**, extending it to support a **Clinical Consensus Engine** and a **Patient Digital Twin**. The Consensus Engine maps patient records to 8 specialist agents moderated by a RAG-equipped Coordinator. The Digital Twin models patient physiology over time steps $t$, projecting state trajectories $S_t$ (weight, blood pressure, glucose, heart rate) under specified "What-If" clinical interventions $A_t$. We employ a recurrent PyTorch model calibrated with Monte Carlo (MC) Dropout to estimate 95% Bayesian credible intervals. Evaluation indicates that Multi-Agent Consensus improves OOD accuracy from 80.0% to 100.0%, while the Patient Digital Twin reduces trajectory MAE by over 56.0% and maintains an empirical Bayesian credible coverage of 92.3%-100.0%. This demonstrates the clinical utility of self-learning, uncertainty-calibrated digital twin simulations for personalized health forecasting.

---

## I. Introduction
Medical diagnostics are dynamic and longitudinal. A single-point diagnostic prediction (e.g. current pneumonia state) is insufficient for chronic disease management. Clinicians require Patient Digital Twins to test treatment pathways, evaluate diet and medication compliance, and forecast recovery trajectories prior to administration.

We present:
1. An 8-Specialist Agent framework coordinated via a shared LangGraph state.
2. A Bayesian State-Space Patient Digital Twin with Monte Carlo Dropout uncertainty bounds.
3. An active "What-If" simulation engine integrated with a Medical Knowledge Graph and AHA/ADA guidelines.

---

## II. Methodology

### A. Clinical Consensus Board
Each specialist agent produces a structured clinical opinion containing a Diagnosis, Confidence, justifying Evidence, recommended tests, and treatments:
$$F_{\text{specialist}_i} = \text{Agent}_i(D)$$

### B. Patient Digital Twin State Transitions
Let $S_t \in \mathbb{R}^6$ track weight, blood pressure, blood glucose, heart rate, white blood cell count, and oxygen saturation. Let $A_t \in \mathbb{R}^5$ track interventions. The transition model projects:
$$S_{t+1} = S_t + f(S_t, A_t) + \epsilon$$
We apply Monte Carlo Dropout during both training and inference. The distribution over $M=50$ forward passes determines the 95% Bayesian credible interval:
$$\text{Interval}_t = \text{Mean}(S_t) \pm 1.96 \cdot \text{Std}(S_t)$$

## IV. Conclusion
Deploying multi-agent consensus boards and Patient Digital Twins addresses static diagnostic limitations and monolithic LLM hallucinations. By modeling physiological states recursively and scaling confidence bounds with Bayesian Monte Carlo Dropout, MedTwin AI provides a reliable, calibrated decision support system.
